## The Ingredients

- 1 pack kinzanji miso-marinated pork loin
- Neutral cooking oil, just a splash
- Sweet soy sauce
- Bok choy, chopped, stems and leaves separated
- Oyster sauce
- 1 egg
- Freshly cracked black pepper
- Hot steamed rice

## The Process

**1. Prep the pork.** Use a butter knife or a spoon to gently scrape the
thickest clumps of miso marinade off the pork slices, so the sugars don't
instantly scorch in the pan.

**2. The slow sear.** Heat a light drizzle of neutral oil in a skillet over
medium-low. Lay the pork down and let it fry slowly. You want the fat to render
and the miso to caramelize into a deep, sticky crust.

**3. The char siu lacquer.** Right before the pork finishes, hit the pan with a
generous drizzle of sweet soy sauce. Let it bubble up and coat the meat for
about 15 seconds. Move the pork to a cutting board, leaving the sticky drippings
in the pan.

**4. The glossy greens.** Keep the heat at medium and toss the bok choy stems
into the leftover miso-soy drippings. Add a tiny splash of water and cover for
30 seconds to steam. Uncover, toss in the leaves, add half a teaspoon of oyster
sauce, and toss until wilted and glossy. Remove from the pan.

**5. The cinematic egg.** Wipe the pan, or grab a small clean one. A little more
oil over medium-high. Fry the egg so the edges go violently crisp and jagged
while the yolk stays completely runny. Finish with freshly cracked black pepper.

## The Assembly

Lay down a generous bed of hot steamed rice. Slice the rested pork into thick
strips and fan them across one side of the bowl. Tuck the umami-glazed bok choy
along the edge. Drop the crispy fried egg dead centre, and pour any juices left
on the cutting board or in the pan right over the top.
