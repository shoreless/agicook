*The Fermented Neon Glaze — no-knife edition.*

A high-heat collision of Javanese street-stall caramel and Tokyo izakaya bite.
Fermented soy cakes fried hard, lacquered in an electric sweet-soy and
citrus-chili tar, buried under a cold, zero-effort heap of store-bought
pre-sliced scallions.

## The Specs

- **The Protein:** 200g tempeh, snapped or quickly sliced into thin bite-sized batons
- **The Lacquer:**
  - 2.5 tbsp sweet soy sauce (*kecap manis*)
  - 1 tsp garlic/ginger paste
  - 1/2 to 1 tsp yuzukosho (for the salty citrus sting)
  - 1 tbsp warm water (crucial barrier so the thick sweet soy doesn't immediately incinerate)
- **The Low-Effort Crown:** a fat, cold fistful of pre-sliced negi straight out of the plastic tub
- **The Fat:** 2 tbsp neutral cooking oil

## Execution

**1. The Crucible.** Set a skillet over medium-high heat with the oil. Drop the
tempeh in a flat layer. Don't touch it for 2 to 3 minutes until a crisp, deep
golden-brown armor forms. Flip and fry for another 2 to 3 minutes until rigid,
nutty, and blistered.

**2. The Slurry.** Stir the sweet soy, garlic/ginger paste, yuzukosho, and water
together in a small bowl or cup.

**3. The Shellac.** Drop the skillet heat to medium-low. Dump in the sauce. It
will violently froth and bubble — stir constantly with tongs or chopsticks for
about 45 to 60 seconds as the steam clears and the glaze turns into a
hyper-glossy, sticky dark lacquer coating every inch of the tempeh. Pull from the
heat instantly.

**4. The Dump.** Tumble the steaming tempeh onto your plate. Crack open that tub
of pre-sliced negi and aggressively dump a cold mountain directly across the top.

Best consumed while burning your tongue, alternating between the sticky,
citrus-salted tempeh and handfuls of chips.
